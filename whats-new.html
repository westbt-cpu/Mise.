barcode scanning that works on iPhone

Scanning quietly did nothing on iPhones — Safari is missing the piece of browser machinery it relied on. That piece is now bundled with the app, and a scanned barcode also looks up the product's name automatically from Open Food Facts, a free grocery database.

tap the numbers to filter

your AI assistant, your choice

mise writes shopping-haul and recipe prompts you paste into an AI chat. The old "open Claude" button was hardwired to one assistant and — because of how it tried to open the app — usually got blocked and did nothing. Now there are two dependable buttons, and settings lets each person pick Claude, ChatGPT, Gemini, Grok, or their own. The prompt itself got smarter too: it now asks the AI for categories, package notes, and expiry dates, and mise files all of it automatically.

the pantry matcher stopped guessing

When a recipe checks your shelf, it has to decide whether "oats" in the recipe means "rolled oats" in your pantry. The old matcher compared fragments of words, which produced confident nonsense — it fixed these by only matching whole words.

aluminum foil ✕ used to match olive oil
steak ✕ used to match green tea
rolled oats ✓ still matches oats

Cooking also now subtracts exactly one package per ingredient it finds — before, two different screens did this math two different ways.

quieter improvements you'll feel

  • A gentler first run. New users are asked whether they'd like a sample kitchen to explore — instead of silently finding groceries they never bought.
  • Undo instead of oops. Deleting from the shopping list shows an undo button for a few seconds.
  • It feels like an app. Little haptic taps, press-and-hold to count quantities fast, sheets you can flick away, and swipe actions that work in both directions.
  • One-tap handoff on iPhone. Optionally, an Apple Shortcut can take a prompt straight to your assistant — one tap instead of copy, switch, paste.
  • Updates actually arrive. A versioning fix means phones pick up new releases on the next open, instead of clinging to an old copy indefinitely.
  • A safety net for the future. The trickiest logic (parsing, matching, cooking math) now lives in small tested modules — 32 automatic checks that catch regressions before users do.

what I deliberately didn't touch

Storage and household sync work exactly as they do today. No accounts, no new services, no changes to where anyone's data lives.
